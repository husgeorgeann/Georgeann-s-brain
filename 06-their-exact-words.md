# Their Exact Words

Rule at the top of this file: never rewrite what a client already said better. This is raw, real language, collected straight from Georgeann. It does not get cleaned up or turned into marketing copy.

## Buyer language

"I know you said that you thought this house was for me, but some may look good on the photos and don't look anything like them. I know you previewed it for me and we have talked enough that I know I have told you what I really want, I just don't want to be disappointed."

This is what a buyer sounds like mid-search: trusting her judgment but still bracing for disappointment. Anything written to a buyer audience should speak directly to that tension between hope and being let down before.

## Seller language

Most of Georgeann's listing appointments come from sellers who have already seen her videos, seen her community involvement, and feel like they know her personally before she ever walks in the door, or who were handed her number by a past client. By the time she is in the car headed to the appointment, the seller has usually already decided they trust her. The sales job happened before the appointment, through video and reputation.

## Objection language

"I really feel like my home is more than your comps."

Her real answer: "I hard comp all of my listings, much like an appraiser would, because ultimately that is what we will be looking at in the future."

This is the pricing objection, and it is the one she hears most. The reframe is not an argument, it is a preview of what the appraisal process will actually look like, delivered before the seller hits that wall on their own.

## Ready-to-move language

"I have been thinking about selling, not actually ready yet but..."

That trailing "but" is the tell. Georgeann hears this sentence, or something close to it, right before someone is actually ready to move forward. Content aimed at sellers on the fence should speak directly into that "not ready yet but" moment.
